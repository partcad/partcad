# Upstarts, not market leaders, unlock PartCAD print ordering

The 3D printing services market in the US and Europe is large, growing and dominated by companies that offer no buyer API. The services worth integrating first are a small set of API-first upstarts. **Craftcloud** (a Munich marketplace of roughly 150–200 print shops across the US and Europe) and **Slant 3D** (an Idaho print farm) are the two to build first. Both publish machine-readable specs, quote and order over plain REST, and need no partner contract. **Materialise OnSite** and **3DAPI** come next: they are the only well-documented services that accept STEP, which is what PartCAD sends today. Additive manufacturing services earned about **$11.7B in 2025, up 15.5%**, roughly half of all AM revenue. Yet the three largest Western online players (Xometry, Protolabs and Fictiv) expose only supplier-side, PunchOut or CAD add-in integrations, which no open-source plugin can use with an ordinary user's credentials. Shapeways, Sculpteo, 3YOURMIND-hosted bureaus and JLC3DP are worth watching but not building on yet, and Treatstock's single undisclosable partner key rules it out. Building any of these plugins will expose more of PartCAD itself than of the vendors. The manufacturer `order` path is a stub that cannot be called. The loader sends only STEP, although three of the four leading candidates prefer STL. And nothing yet maps a provider's material names onto `//pub/std/manufacturing/material`.

## A $11.7B services market whose leaders keep their APIs closed

Services have overtaken machines as the centre of additive manufacturing. Wohlers Report 2026 values the whole AM market at **$24.2B in 2025 (+10.9%)**. Of that, **services were $11.7B (+15.5%), about 48%**, while printer sales grew only 3.6% ([3D Printing Industry](https://3dprintingindustry.com/news/am-sector-revenue-reaches-24-2bn-as-growth-shifts-from-machines-to-services-249373/)). AM Research counts more narrowly but shows the same trend: services reached **~$2.50B in Q2 2026, up 14% year on year**, more than half of a $4.48B quarter ([3DPrint.com](https://3dprint.com/333786/3d-printing-services-near-2-5b-as-am-market-shifts-beyond-hardware/amp/)). No reachable source splits services revenue between the US and Europe. Wohlers' regional growth rates still point one way: **12.6% in the Americas against 9.0% in EMEA** (Asia-Pacific led at 19.8%) ([3Dnatives](https://www.3dnatives.com/en/wohlers-report-2026-17022026/)). The figures from generalist market-research firms ($5–7B "service bureau" markets growing at 18%) use undisclosed definitions and are best ignored. Most of this revenue is negotiated industrial contract work. Nobody sizes the slice that matters to PartCAD, online instant-quote ordering of prototypes and short runs, on its own.

The leaders of that online slice are American and growing fast. **Xometry** reported **$252.4M in Q3 2026 (+40%)**, its first GAAP-profitable quarter and a run rate above $1B. **$213.4M of that was US revenue against $39.0M international**, and international grew more slowly ([Xometry Q3 2026 release](https://www.financialcontent.com/article/gnwcq-2026-11-3-xometry-reports-record-third-quarter-2026-results)). **Protolabs** booked a record $154.3M in the same quarter, but only **$22.4M (about 14.5%) came from 3D printing**; CNC and moulding dominate ([Protolabs 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001443669/000144366926000043/prlb-20261029xexx991.htm)). **Fictiv** now belongs to MISUMI, which paid $350M in 2025 ([TCT Magazine](https://www.tctmagazine.com/fictiv-officially-acquired-by-misumi/)). The European side is smaller and flatter. Materialise's Manufacturing segment earned **€47.1M in H1 2026, down 1.2%**, and its growth now comes from aerospace and defence rather than online orders ([Barchart](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/4173019/materialise-reports-second-quarter-and-half-year-2026-results)).

Europe's consumer-facing brands spent 2024–2026 being closed, merged or rebuilt. **i.materialise stopped taking orders on 31 October 2025** and folded into OnSite; its old API now returns 404 ([VoxelMatters](https://www.voxelmatters.com/i-materialise-merges-into-materialise-onsite-3d-printing-service/)). **Shapeways filed Chapter 7 in July 2024** and was relaunched from Eindhoven in December 2024 as a B2B and creator print-on-demand business ([3DPrint.com](https://3dprint.com/314952/shapeways-relaunched-with-all-assets-under-one-roof/?noamp=mobile)). **Sculpteo left BASF and merged into France's 3D Prod in May 2026** ([TCT Magazine](https://www.tctmagazine.com/explained-why-3d-prod-sculpteo-have-merged/)). The analysts' summary of 2025 was that it closed "the chapter on the first generation of publicly traded AM startups" ([3DPrint.com](https://3dprint.com/322764/who-merged-who-was-acquired-and-who-disappeared-in-3d-printing-in-2025/)). For an integrator, the lesson is that brand, URL and API continuity is usually the first casualty even when the business survives. That argues for PartCAD's existing design, in which each provider lives in its own repository and the index pins it, so a dead backend costs one index entry and nothing in PartCAD.

The market serves three customer groups, and PartCAD's users sit in two of them. Engineers prototyping and building short runs buy from Xometry, Protolabs, Fictiv and Craftcloud. Makers and creators buy from Craftcloud, Shapeways, Slant 3D and the Chinese services. The only cross-service price comparison is All3DP's, and All3DP shares a parent company with Craftcloud, so its numbers need that caveat. For one sample part it found **Craftcloud $37.40, Xometry $68.54 and Protolabs Network $90.00** ([All3DP](https://all3dp.com/2/best-cheap-3d-printing-services/)). Trade policy divides the two markets in a way that matters for the Chinese services. Since March 2026, JLC3DP pre-collects **40% duty on plastic parts and 70% on 316L steel** for US buyers, and US individuals under $800 have been forced onto prepaid-duty shipping since May 2025 ([JLC3DP tariff FAQ](https://jlc3dp.com/help/article/us-tariff-policy-faq)). Those tariffs do not apply in Europe, so JLC3DP stays a plausibly cheap option for European users, though no EU-side data confirmed that.

## Only six services take a quote and an order from outside code

API availability runs almost exactly opposite to market share. Xometry's developer portal is a **supplier-side API for its manufacturing partners**: accepted jobs, part files, line-item statuses and job-board offers, with access requested by email. It has no endpoint for quoting or ordering ([Xometry Developer API](https://developer.xometry.com/docs/getting-started)). Its buyer integrations (add-ins for SOLIDWORKS, Fusion, Onshape and Inventor, plus enterprise PunchOut) run on a private API ([Xometry CAD add-ins](https://xometry.com/cad-add-ins)). Protolabs' 2026 ProDesk platform is browser-only ([Protolabs press release](https://protolabs.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/protolabs-introduces-prodesk-ai-enabled-manufacturing-platform)). Protolabs Network (formerly Hubs), Fictiv, Stratasys Direct, Quickparts, Weerg and Laserhub likewise offer web quoting only; Weerg's own `llms.txt` tells agents to send users to its configurator ([Weerg llms.txt](https://www.weerg.com/llms.txt)). The white-label quoting platforms many bureaus run on (Paperless Parts, DigiFabster, AMFG) expose their APIs to the shop, not to the shop's customers ([Paperless Parts API](https://paperlessparts.com/api)).

What remains is a handful of services built for programmatic buyers. Several now advertise themselves to AI agents. Slant 3D's OpenAPI spec says it "is for automated tools and AI Agents" ([Slant 3D OpenAPI](https://slant3dapi.com/v2/api/openapi.json)), Slant runs a public MCP server ([Slant 3D MCP](https://www.slant3d.com/mcp-server)), 3DAPI ships an MCP server and an Arazzo workflow document ([3DAPI llms.txt](https://public-api.dapi.digital/docs/llms.txt)), and Kiln built a Craftcloud adapter for agents in February 2026 ([Kiln](https://www.kiln3d.com/blog/craftcloud-live)). The research turned up no print-service integration in any open-source code-CAD tool (FreeCAD, OpenSCAD, CadQuery, build123d), though it did not search those ecosystems systematically. If that holds, PartCAD would be early.

## Craftcloud and Slant 3D are the two to build first

**Craftcloud** gives PartCAD users the most from one plugin: engineering processes (SLS, MJF, SLA, metal) and consumer FDM, sourced from shops on both sides of the Atlantic, in the buyer's own currency. Its quote path runs headless end to end. Prices arrive per vendor, the cart response already carries per-vendor shipping lines and totals, and the order creates a hosted **Stripe Checkout session whose URL** the user opens to pay ([Craftcloud spec](https://api.craftcloud3d.com/api-docs.json)). That suits a PartCAD with no payment infrastructure. Three uncertainties come with it. Quote and order need no credentials at all, so it meets "never a shared key" trivially, but the user's identity is only the email and addresses sent with the order. The spec's material catalogue lives on a separate service behind Cloudflare, which leaves `caps` the weakest of the three calls. And no published terms explicitly permit third-party clients, although Kiln, HP's MJF portal and several public GitHub adapters run on the same API, which shows such clients are tolerated in practice.

**Slant 3D** fits PartCAD's contract most exactly, and it is the cheapest to build and test against. Each user creates their own bearer key. A draft order's `publicId` serves directly as the cart ID, and `POST /orders/{publicId}` "begin[s] production and charge[s] payment" to the card on file ([Slant 3D OpenAPI](https://slant3dapi.com/v2/api/openapi.json)). A community mock server simulates the whole order flow and its webhooks, so continuous integration (CI) never has to spend money ([GitHub search](https://github.com/search?q=slant3d&type=repositories)). Its limits are just as clear: STL only, FDM only, USD only, and production in the US. The spec does not settle whether its API quotes delivery outside the US. Slant 3D raised its first round only in April 2025 ([Fabbaloo](https://www.fabbaloo.com/?p=229681)), so it carries the counterparty risk of a small private company.

The "build next" pair serves the users the first pair does not. **Materialise OnSite** maps onto `caps`/`quote`/`order` almost one to one, accepts STEP, and returns an honest quote: parts price, transport, VAT and minimum order, each with its currency ([OnSite swagger](https://api.onsite.materialise.com/documentation/v1/swagger.json)). Its credentials are per user, but each account must be API-enabled by an account manager, and ordering needs invoice payment enabled ([OnSite help](https://onsite.helpjuice.com/api-connection/order-submitting-using-api)). That limits it in practice to companies, the professional European buyers Craftcloud's maker shops serve least well. **3DAPI** is the one candidate that works with PartCAD's STEP-only loader today, and its money and idempotency conventions are the cleanest found ([3DAPI llms.txt](https://public-api.dapi.digital/docs/llms.txt)). Against it: it is FDM-only on Bambu printers, the company's health and domicile are unknown, the exact quote and order paths were not confirmed, and a casual user must clear a sample-file review and supply business billing details ([3DAPI business guide](https://public-api.dapi.digital/docs/guides/integrator-business.md)).

The "watch" tier is held back by a fact still to be confirmed, not by technology. **Shapeways** has the best authentication model of all, per-user OAuth2 with an `oob` redirect for command-line tools ([Shapeways quick start](https://developers.shapeways.com/quick-start)). But its discovery document still reports revision "20121123", and nothing says the post-bankruptcy company supports API orders. One email to api@shapeways.com would settle it. **3YOURMIND** is strategically interesting because every bureau running it serves the same API on its own domain. A single plugin, parameterised by tenant URL, would let the public index list each bureau as one entry. Tokens come from the bureau or 3YOURMIND support, though, and the live `/apidocs/` could not be read. **Sculpteo** works only for partners on invoice terms and has just changed owner. **JLC3DP** gates access on order history. Treatstock's partner key fails PartCAD's credential rule outright.

## Five PartCAD gaps stand between a quote and a delivered part

The largest gap is on PartCAD's side. **Nothing in PartCAD can place an order yet.** `pc supply order` is an empty command whose body is four TODOs (validation, order processing, error handling, confirmation). `ProviderRequestOrder.__init__` raises `NotImplementedError("Order request is not implemented yet.")`, and no code calls a provider's `query_order` (`src/partcad_cli/click/commands/supply/order.py`, `src/partcad/plugin_request_provider_order.py`). Before any plugin's `order` matters, PartCAD needs the request that carries a cart ID to the right provider. It also needs a result that can say "pay here", because Craftcloud and 3DAPI's checkout mode answer with a URL rather than a charge, and a confirmation step, because every `order` here spends real money with no sandbox behind it. Craftcloud's `isTestOrder` flag and Slant's mock server are the only safe ways to exercise that path.

The second gap is geometry. The manufacturer loader renders the part, or the design behind a printed part, to STEP and attaches it. **For any provider whose `caps` lacks `step`, it logs an error and sends nothing** (`load()` in `src/partcad/plugin_factory_provider_manufacturer.py`). Of the candidates above, Craftcloud (in every documented example) and Slant 3D (by schema) need STL, and Treatstock accepts STL, PLY or 3MF but not STEP. The fix is small but carries decisions. The loader should walk the provider's `formats` in order of preference, export STL or 3MF from the same design geometry `_sent_geometry` already selects, and state its unit (Craftcloud takes `mm`, and Shapeways needs `uploadScale` 0.001 for millimetre STL). The tessellation tolerance should be chosen for printing, not for viewing.

Third, **materials have no shared vocabulary**. `is_part_available` compares a cart item's material, colour and finish as plain names against what `caps` returns. But Slant names a filament profile and an opaque filament ID, OnSite a nested set of UUIDs, and Craftcloud a `materialConfigId` resolved through a catalogue it does not publish. Each plugin therefore needs a curated table from provider IDs to `//pub/std/manufacturing/material` references, with colours (Slant's hex values are a gift here) and finishes (OnSite's "finishings"). The incubator's `_shared/distributor.py` toolkit set the precedent of one shared toolkit, copied into each repository and held to it by a digest. A `_shared` print-service toolkit holding that mapping, the polling helpers and the result shape would keep four plugins from making four different choices.

Fourth, **a quote is not a single number** for these services, while PartCAD's quote result is essentially `price`, `currency` and `cartId`. `with_currency` already refuses to take a price without its currency, which every candidate can satisfy (`src/partcad/plugin_provider.py`). But uploads and prices are asynchronous everywhere (Craftcloud answers 206 while parsing, Slant needs a confirm step, OnSite a status poll), which pushes against `plugin_query_timeout`. Carts expire (Craftcloud's `expiresAt`, 3DAPI's 24-hour link). VAT and transport come back separately (OnSite) or are excluded (Shapeways' pre-tax USD). Platforms like 3YOURMIND can route a part to manual review and return no automatic price at all ([3YOURMIND Confluence](https://3yourmind.atlassian.net/wiki/x/jwDZBg)). The result shape should grow fields for expiry, tax and shipping breakdown, and a "pending" outcome before the first plugin bakes in its own. The cart's existing `qos` field is the natural carrier for speed tiers such as Craftcloud's express shipping, Shapeways' `manufacturingSpeed` and OnSite's FastLane.

Fifth, **the index has no home for manufacturers**, and the credential story needs one decision. Today the index's `svc/commerce/` holds only store providers, so the first manufacturer entry sets the layout, for example a sibling `svc/manufacturing/` directory; that name is a suggestion, not something the notes or the index settle. Addresses are already handled: manufacturer plugins, and only they, receive the user's `shippingAddress` and `billingAddress` (`src/partcad/plugin_factory_python.py`). Credentials fit `pc login` as the distributor plugins use it. Slant and 3DAPI keys, and OnSite client credentials, are pasted per user, and Shapeways would need the same OAuth2 flow DigiKey's plugin already uses (`incubator/README.md`). Craftcloud needs nothing, and PartCAD should say plainly that its orders are guest orders tied to an email address.

## Conclusion

The market's money and the market's APIs sit in different places. Xometry and Protolabs take most of the online revenue and grow fastest, yet offer an open-source tool nothing to call. The services that do publish specs are small, young, often agent-oriented, and in Europe recently restructured. PartCAD should therefore treat print services as replaceable. Ship Craftcloud for reach and Slant 3D as the reference implementation, keep each in its own index-pinned repository, and expect at least one of them to change shape within two years, as Hubs, i.materialise and Shapeways all did. The agent-facing turn these vendors are taking (MCP servers, `llms.txt`, Arazzo workflows) also gives PartCAD an opening. Its `caps`/`quote`/`order` contract is a candidate for the lightweight "quote this part" protocol the market lacks; the only formal standard found, Catena-X CX-0129, covers just the request half and requires dataspace onboarding ([CX-0129](https://catenax-ev.github.io/docs/next/standards/CX-0129-RequestforQuotationExchange)).

The practical order of work follows from where the friction lies. First implement the order path, payment-URL results and STL export in PartCAD itself: the missing order path blocks every candidate, and the STEP-only loader blocks both of the build-first services. Then build the Slant 3D plugin against its mock server and the Craftcloud plugin beside it, together with a shared material-mapping toolkit. Send one email each to Shapeways and to a 3YOURMIND-hosted bureau before deciding whether the "watch" tier is worth code. Xometry is worth a conversation, not a plugin: if a partner integration ever opens, it is the single change that would most widen what a PartCAD user can order.
